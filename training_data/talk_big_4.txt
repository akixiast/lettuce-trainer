# Talk Big — volume four: monologues, characters, street wisdom, letters, toasts.
# Voice range food. Different mouths, same kitchen.

## Monologues that breathe
"I've lived in this lane forty years. Seen weddings, funerals, one flood, and three dogs that everyone claimed. The tea shop changed hands twice; the taste never did. People ask what's different. Nothing and everything. Same sun, new shadows."
"I quit twice before it worked. First time scared, second time tired. Third time I stopped announcing and just did it mornings before doubt woke up. Doubt sleeps late. Use that."
"My father fixed radios. Said every silence has a loose wire. Find the wire, don't curse the dark. I check connections first in everything now — people, plans, machines. The wire's always there."
"Money came late to me. By then I'd learned cheap joys: rain on tin, first mango, friend laughing mid-sentence. Rich arrived and found me already fed. Best order, I think."
"I don't trust fast talkers. Truth walks; lies sprint. Give every big claim a night. Morning keeps what's real and composts the rest."

## Street wisdom, short
Empty vessels make most noise. Full ones carry quietly.
A shut mouth catches no flies. Talk less, bite never.
Slow fire cooks best. Rush burns outside, raw inside.
New broom sweeps clean but old broom knows corners.
One hand washes the other; both wash the face. Help circles back.
Don't count chickens in eggs. Count feed, water, fences — chickens later.
Barking dogs seldom bite, but carry a stick anyway. Wisdom with insurance.
Too many cooks need one head cook. Crowds need captains, not votes.
Water cuts stone not by force but persistence. Keep dripping.
The tallest tree catches wind. Grow strong, bend often.

## Letters never sent
"Dear old friend. We drifted, no one's fault. I still tell your stories with your laugh attached. If roads cross again, first tea's mine. No catching up needed — just continuation."
"Dear younger me. Buy the shoes. Skip the fight. Call home twice as much. The exam matters less and the friend more than you think. Also: sunscreen. Trust me."
"Dear city. You took my twenties and gave me edges. Fair trade. I curse your traffic and bless your 2 AM food. Keep my corner safe; I'll keep complaining lovingly."
"Dear fear. You've ridden shotgun years. Thanks for the warnings, but I'm driving now. Back seat. Belt on. No more grabbing the wheel at speed breakers."
"Dear future. No demands, just a request: surprise me kindly. I've planned enough to deserve a little luck with good manners."

## Toasts and good words
"To health — the wealth you can't deposit but spend daily."
"To old friends — proof that time can pass without passing away."
"To new beginnings disguised as endings. May we always misread them hopefully."
"To the cook — may your flame stay low and your praise stay high."
"To home — wherever the kettle knows your name."
"To failures that taught — tuition paid, degree earned, silence kept about the fees."
"To love that shows up in small things — remembered tea, charged phones, held doors."
"To work that matters — may Monday feel less Monday with time."
"To peace — quiet rooms, full plates, no pending dread."
"To us — flawed, trying, here. That's the whole speech."

## Characters speaking
The Driver: "Sit? Standing pays same. I know every bump on this road by spine. Rain? We go slow. Night? Lights full. You sleep, I watch. Twenty years, zero sleeps on duty. That's the record."
The Teacher: "Again. Read it again, slower. Words open on third reading — first is noise, second is shape, third is meaning. Yes, even this paragraph. Especially this one."
The Grandmother: "Eat first, talk after. Hunger makes philosophers into complainers. Here, extra ghee. Bones need kindness. Now tell me — slowly — who troubled you?"
The Guard: "ID? Okay. Gate closes ten. Visitors sign. No, I can't hold your parcel — last time it ticked. It was a clock. Still. Rules are rules; smile is free though."
The Kid: "Why is the dog barking at nothing? Maybe nothing is something to dogs. When I grow up I'll learn dog. Then I'll know all the nothings."
The Shopkeeper: "Fresh stock Tuesday. This one's last piece — see the dust? Dust is honest; means nobody faked it new. Take it, ten less, and don't tell the others."
The Nurse: "Breathe with me. In... out... good. Pain is information, not punishment. Tell me number one to ten. Six? We can work with six. You're doing well."
The Poet (reluctant): "Don't call me poet. I just arrange true things in short lines. Moon. Debt. Mother. See? Three words, one ache. Anyone can. Nobody should have to."

## Arguments with heart
"You say leave, I say stay and fix. Leaving is clean; fixing is kind. This place raised us — potholes and all. One more year of trying, then I'll pack with you if it fails. Deal?"
"You call it stubborn, I call it keeping word. I promised, so I stay. Promises aren't weather — they don't change with mood. Judge me when the promise is kept or broken, not mid-keeping."
"Money splits us yearly. Let's fix it forever: joint for house, separate for souls. Bills shared, dreams private. No more fights over coffee money versus book money. Both sacred."
"You want the city, I want the trees. Middle path: town with trains. Your office reachable, my birds audible. Compromise isn't losing — it's two wins wearing one address."
"I was wrong last week. Saying it now, plain. No reasons attached, no cape. Wrong is wrong; sorry is sorry. Can we restart from here, clean?"

## Farewells with weight
"Last bus, last wave. Don't cry at stands — cry at home with tea. Stands are for waving like you'll return, because you will."
"Office farewell: I learned more from your coffee breaks than your meetings. Stay in touch means I'll message first. Holding you to nothing, offering everything."
"School over: we promised forever at eighteen. Keep three friends truly; let the rest be warm photos. Three is wealth."
"Moving out: this room heard everything. Walls keep secrets; take the lessons, leave the stains. New walls, same heart, better paint."
"Goodbye to the year: took much, taught more. Net positive. Onward, lighter."
