# World Knowledge — facts across science, history, body, earth, space, machines.
# Feeds knowledge lanes. Plain sentences, one idea each, true to 2026 best knowledge.

## The human body
The heart beats about 100,000 times a day, pushing 7,500 liters of blood.
Muscles pull; they never push. Every movement is a team of pulls.
Taste buds live two weeks, then replaced. Burnt tongue heals fast by design.
Bones break down and rebuild constantly; a full skeleton refreshes in about ten years.
The brain runs on 20 watts — a dim bulb's power for the whole mind.
Eyes adjust to darkness in 30 minutes; full night vision is slow chemistry.
Skin is the largest organ. It sheds 30,000 cells a minute, quietly.
Hearing never sleeps fully; the brain just turns the volume down at night.
Fingernails grow four times faster than toenails. Use drives growth.
Laughter relaxes muscles and drops stress hormones measurably.

## Earth
Rivers carve valleys grain by grain; the Grand Canyon is patience made visible.
Deserts were oceans once. Whale bones rest in Saharan sand.
Volcanoes breathe before erupting — ground swells, gases shift, animals leave.
Earthquakes mostly ride plate edges; the ground slips, waves run, buildings sway.
Soil is alive: a spoon holds more microbes than people on Earth.
Mountains grow millimeters a year and wash down just as slowly.
Lightning heats air five times hotter than the sun's surface, briefly.
Clouds weigh tons — thousands — yet float on rising warm air.
The deep sea is Earth's largest habitat and least explored; maps of Mars beat maps of seabeds.
Seasons come from tilt, not distance. Summer is leaning toward fire, winter away.

## Space
Light from the sun is eight minutes old when it touches skin.
Mars sunsets are blue. Dust scatters evening the other way around.
A day on Venus outlasts its year. It spins backward, slowly, under crushing air.
Jupiter's storm is wider than Earth and has raged centuries.
Saturn would float in a big enough ocean — lowest density of all planets.
Neutron stars spin hundreds of times a second; a teaspoon weighs mountains.
Black holes evaporate, slowly, over eons. Nothing escapes, but everything fades.
The universe expands faster each day; distant galaxies cross beyond reach forever.
Gold comes from colliding dead stars. Every ring is ancient violence, cooled.
Astronauts grow taller in space — spines uncompress without gravity, then settle back.

## History, compressed
Fire tamed first: cooking unlocked calories, calories grew brains.
Farming tied people to land; land needed defending; defense needed kings.
Writing began as receipts — clay tokens for grain and goats before poems.
The wheel rolled five thousand years ago; roads followed, then empires.
Paper from China, numbers from India, lenses from everywhere — ideas travel faster than armies.
Plagues rewrote wages: fewer workers meant survivors earned more.
Printing pressed books cheap; cheap books pressed minds; minds pressed revolutions.
Steam shrank distance; telegraph killed it. News outran horses forever after.
Flight took eleven years from first hop to crossing war skies.
The internet began as military mail, became everyone's market, library, and street.

## Machines and code
Computers count in twos: on, off, billions of flickers per second.
Programs are recipes; bugs are typos with consequences.
The first programmer wrote before computers existed — poetry of gears, 1843.
Networks split messages into packets; each finds its own road; they reunite at the door.
Encryption is math locks: easy to shut, hard to pick without the key.
Databases remember everything and forgive nothing; backups forgive on their behalf.
Phones sense touch by electric fields; gloves block the whisper.
GPS needs four satellites minimum: three for place, one for time.
Batteries age with heat and full charges; cool and half-full lives longest.
Robots repeat perfectly and improvise never — the opposite of people, for now.

## Food and kitchen science
Salt pulls water out; sugar pulls water in. Both preserve by thirst.
Bread rises on gas: yeast eats sugar, breathes bubbles, dough inflates.
Cold sharpens knives' work — chilled onions release less tear gas.
Acid cooks without heat: lime firms fish in ceviche, chemistry as flame.
Caramel is burnt sugar stopped in time — seconds between sweet and bitter.
Eggs set at different heats: whites firm first, yolks follow lazy.
Spices fade in light and air; dark jars keep their voice years longer.
Rice doubles by water math: one cup dry, two water, patience, lid.
Fermentation is controlled rot: kimchi, curd, bread, beer — rot with a job.
Taste is smell's junior partner; blocked noses flatten feasts.

## Money sense
Spend less than arrives. The whole religion in one line.
Emergency funds first: three months of must-spends before any investing.
Compound interest is slow magic: small, early, untouched beats big, late, fiddled.
Debt with high interest is fire; pay the hottest fire first.
Track for a month before budgeting; guesses lie, receipts don't.
Insurance buys sleep, not profit. Cover ruin, skip the rest.
Skills compound like money: daily reps beat weekend marathons.
Buy tools that earn, rent toys that don't. Assets feed, liabilities eat.
Inflation taxes the mattress; idle cash melts yearly, quietly.
Rich is owing nothing and sleeping well — the rest is scoreboard.

## Health basics
Sleep glues memory; all-nighters borrow from tomorrow at cruel interest.
Walk daily; the cheapest medicine has no prescription line.
Water before coffee; the night dries you, mornings repay.
Protein rebuilds; vegetables regulate; sugar excites then abandons.
Sunlight sets the body clock; mornings outside beat pills for rhythm.
Strength twice weekly keeps bones honest past forty.
Breathing slow tells nerves the war is over; six breaths a minute calms storms.
Checkups catch whispers before screams; yearly once, non-negotiable past thirty.
Screens steal sleep an hour each night on average; charge phones outside bedrooms.
Friends heal: loneliness harms like smoking, company repairs like rest.

## Nature notes
Trees talk through fungal wires, trading sugar and warnings underground.
Wolves reshape rivers: fewer deer, more saplings, steadier banks — trophic cascades.
Bees dance directions; the angle to the sun is the map, duration is distance.
Ants farm fungus and herd aphids; tiny civilizations under every stone.
Migrating birds read stars, magnets, and smell; losing one sense, they use the rest.
Coral bleaches when seas warm two degrees too long; color returns if coolness returns.
Mushrooms are fruit; the real body webs soil for miles, oldest and largest alive.
Rainforests make their own rain; cut them, skies dry, rivers thin.
Crows hold grudges and funerals; they remember faces for years.
Seeds wait decades for one good rain; patience is a survival strategy.

## Thinking tools
Correlation is not cause: ice cream and drowning rise together — summer causes both.
Sample size matters: three wins prove nothing, three hundred start to.
Base rates first: rare diseases need strong tests, or positives lie mostly.
Opportunity cost: every yes is a thousand quiet nos. Choose accordingly.
Second-order effects: the fix causes the next problem; think two moves deep.
Incentives rule: show me the reward, I'll show you the behavior.
Occam's razor: simplest fitting story usually wins; extra parts need proof.
Survivorship bias: winners' advice skips the graveyard of identical tries.
Sunk cost: spent money is gone; decide by future, never by past pain.
Falsify, don't confirm: seek the fact that kills your idea; survivors deserve trust.
