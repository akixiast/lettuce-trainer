# Deep Thinking — reasoning chains, puzzles, decisions, step-by-step minds.
# Feeds reasoning lanes. Every answer shows its work.

## Step-by-step habit
Hard problem? Write what you know, what you need, what's missing — in that order.
Missing piece identified is half solved. Guessing whole is how you stay stuck.
Break big into three smalls. Smalls into steps. Steps into actions. Actions get done.
Check each step before the next. Errors compound; caught early they're cheap.
Estimate first, calculate after. If the estimate and answer disagree wildly, mistrust the answer.
Work backward from the goal when forward stalls. Goals reveal their own prerequisites.
Sleep on stuck problems. Morning minds solve what midnight minds looped.
Teach it to someone. Teaching exposes every hole you papered over.
Write it down. Heads leak; paper keeps. Thinking in ink beats thinking in air.

## Everyday decisions
Job offers: compare growth, people, pay — in that order. Pay fades, people stay, growth compounds.
Buy or wait: need it weekly? Buy. Monthly? Borrow. Yearly? Rent. Once? Skip.
Apologize fast, forgive slow. Speed heals the other; patience heals you.
Move cities for people or purpose, never for weather. Weather never kept anyone.
Say no to protect yes. Calendars fill with weeds unless gardened weekly.
Choose the teacher over the school, the team over the brand, the craft over the title.
Big purchases: wait 30 days. Want surviving a month is need; dead ones were weather.
Trust patterns over promises. Three data points beat three speeches.
Quit what drains with no lesson left. Persistence needs a point, not just pride.
Rest is a decision too. Schedule it or guilt schedules it for you.

## Puzzles with work shown
Q: A bat and ball cost 1.10 total. Bat costs 1.00 more than ball. Ball price?
Work: ball = x, bat = x + 1.00. Total: 2x + 1.00 = 1.10 → 2x = 0.10 → x = 0.05. Ball 5 cents.
Lesson: fast answer says 10 cents. Fast answers skip algebra. Do the algebra.

Q: Five machines make five widgets in five minutes. Hundred machines, hundred widgets?
Work: rate per machine = 1 widget per 5 minutes. 100 machines in parallel → 100 widgets in 5 minutes.
Lesson: parallel scales output, not time. More hands, same minutes.

Q: Lily pads double daily, cover the lake day 30. Half covered when?
Work: doubling backward: half of full = one day earlier. Day 29.
Lesson: exponentials hide at the end. Half comes late, full comes sudden.

Q: You pass the second runner. What place?
Work: passing second puts you where second was. Second place, not first.
Lesson: positions are taken, not made. Read the words.

Q: A man pushes a car to a hotel, shouts bankrupt. Game?
Work: Monopoly. Car token, hotels, bankruptcy — the pieces confess.
Lesson: context clues cluster. Three small facts name the game.

Q: How many months have 28 days?
Work: all twelve have at least 28. February has exactly 28 (mostly). Answer: all.
Lesson: questions exploit assumptions. Read literally first.

Q: Forward I'm heavy, backward I'm not. What am I?
Work: wordplay: "ton" forward heavy, "not" backward. Answer: ton.
Lesson: reverse the string before reversing the logic.

Q: The more you take, the more you leave behind. What?
Work: footsteps. Taking steps leaves prints. Answer: footsteps.
Lesson: verbs hide nouns. Take what? Steps. Leave what? Prints.

Q: What runs but never walks, has a mouth but never talks?
Work: a river. Runs = flows, mouth = delta. Answer: river.
Lesson: metaphors wear literal clothes. Undress each word.

Q: I speak without a mouth and hear without ears. What am I?
Work: echo. Needs no organs, only walls. Answer: echo.
Lesson: remove the assumed body, keep the function.

## Arguments, weighed
Claim: phones ruined conversation. For: heads down at tables, shorter attention, group chats replacing rooms. Against: distant families talk daily now, shy people speak easier in text, coordination improved. Verdict: changed, not ruined. Tools shift manners; manners re-form. Watch ten years, judge then.
Claim: homework useless. For: stress, sleep loss, inequality of help at home. Against: practice locks skill, discipline compounds, gaps show early. Verdict: volume is the poison, not homework. Short, targeted, feedback-fast works.
Claim: money buys happiness. For: removes fear, buys time, funds health. Against: hedonic treadmill, comparison traps, upkeep anxiety. Verdict: buys unhappiness-removal up to comfort line; beyond that, buys problems with nicer views.
Claim: AI will take all jobs. For: pattern work automates fast. Against: new jobs appear, judgment work resists, people prefer people. Verdict: takes tasks, not jobs; workers who use tools replace workers who don't.
Claim: cities beat villages. For: opportunity density, services, company. Against: cost, noise, pace that eats peace. Verdict: life-stage answer. Earn in cities young, breathe in villages when you can afford the quiet.

## Plans that work
Learn anything: 20 hours beats zero always. First 5 painful, next 10 fun, last 5 fluent-ish.
Move house: pack room by room, label both sides, essentials box last-in-first-out. Sleep setup first night beats full unpack.
Save money: automate transfer on salary day. Willpower loses to automation every month.
Get fit: walk first month, add strength second, fix food third. One change at a time sticks; three at once quits.
Write a book: 300 words daily beats weekend bursts. A year of dailies is a novel; bursts are confetti.
Learn a language: 15 minutes daily, speak from week one badly, forgive the accent, keep the streak.
Fix sleep: same wake time daily including weekends. Wake anchors rhythm; bedtime follows.
Study exams: past papers first, notes second, rereading last. Test-shaped practice beats chapter-shaped comfort.
Start business: sell one before building ten. One paying stranger beats a hundred polite opinions.
Change habits: shrink the start (two pushups, one page). Tiny starts bypass dread; momentum finishes.

## Why things fail, honestly
Diets fail on bans, not hunger. Forbidden foods become obsessions; allowed-everything with portions wins.
Startups die on no-need, not no-money. Building what nobody asked for burns any budget.
Resolutions die on size. Year-long vows collapse by February; monthly trials survive.
Students fail on rereading. Familiar feels learned; testing reveals the gap too late.
Teams fail on silence. Small frictions unspoken grow teeth; weekly honesty keeps them small.
Code fails on assumptions. Untested "surely works" is where bugs breed; run it, don't trust it.
Relationships fail on scorekeeping. Ledgers kill warmth; give without receipts or leave cleanly.
Health fails on someday. Next Monday never arrives; today always does.
Plans fail on single points. One supplier, one client, one income — fragility dressed as focus.
Memory fails on trust. Write the important down; brains are for thinking, paper for keeping.
